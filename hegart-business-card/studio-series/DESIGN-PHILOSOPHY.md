# Stille Handschrift

*Quiet Signature: design notes for the five-card series*

**Paper first.** Each card is a small object that someone holds in their hand, so the space is laid out the way a bookbinder would lay out a page: generous margins, one clear focal point, and empty space that is left alone on purpose. The type is meant to feel set by hand, not just placed on a grid. Every distance is decided by eye and corrected again, until the composition looks like the result of patient, careful work.

**Two voices.** A display serif with an optical size axis carries the warmth: tall and confident in upright weight, then softened by a single italic word in an accent colour. Next to it, a plain grotesque handles the facts, with a monospaced face used only for tiny labels in uppercase. These three never compete. Each has its own job, and that division should feel the way it does in work by experienced typographers.

**Colour is rare.** A deep night navy, a warm cream like paper and an ink close to black make up the stage. A clear sea blue and a mint green show up only as accents, never more than one per side and never in large areas. No gradients, no glow, no ornamental effects: the colour is printed flat and confidently, like a good offset print.

**The photograph as proof.** When an image appears, it is the studio's own work, cropped with intent, at the edge or in a single frame. It is never decoration; it is the argument. The image carries the emotion, the type keeps quiet next to it. The crop, the proportion and the border are chosen with the care of a printer who checks every proof.

**The mark as architecture.** The monogram is drawn from straight lines and right angles, so it gives the grid for everything else: the lines, the columns and the distances come from its geometry. Its short bar, the only coloured accent of the mark, echoes the accent of the typography. The whole series has to read as one hand: different cards, the same quiet and the same insistence on craft.
